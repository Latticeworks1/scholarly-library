# Humanities

Magic, esotericism, and philosophy, with an emphasis on how ideas moved between Arabic, Greek, Latin, Hebrew, and Slavic traditions.

## Magic and the occult

[The New Age of Russia: Occult and Esoteric Dimensions](2012_Menzel_Hagemeister_Rosenthal_eds_The_New_Age_of_Russia_Occult_and_Esoteric_Dimensions.pdf) (2012), edited by Birgit Menzel, Michael Hagemeister, and Bernice Glatzer Rosenthal. Essays on occult and esoteric movements in Russia from their prerevolutionary roots, through the Soviet period (the occultist Barchenko and the secret police, Nicholas Roerich, Tsiolkovskii and the occult roots of Soviet space travel, cosmism), to the post-Soviet revival (Aleksandr Dugin, the Rodnoverie movement, shamanism, the Roerich movements).

[Unlocked Books: Manuscripts of Learned Magic in the Medieval Libraries of Central Europe](2008_Lang_Unlocked_Books_Manuscripts_of_Learned_Magic_in_the_Medieval_Libraries_of_Central_Europe.pdf) (2008), Benedek Láng, Penn State University Press, Magic in History series. Presents and analyzes texts of learned magic written in medieval Poland, Bohemia, and Hungary and tries to identify their authors and readers. This copy is a 40-page excerpt.

[Amulets and Talismans](Budge_Amulets_and_Talismans_Original_Texts_and_Translations_1961_Edition.pdf), Sir E. A. Wallis Budge (University Books, 1961 edition of the 1930 work, originally titled Amulets and Superstitions). The original texts, with translations and descriptions, of Egyptian, Sumerian, Assyrian, Hebrew, Christian, Gnostic, and Muslim amulets and magical figures, with chapters on the evil eye, the origin of the amulet, the pentagon, the swastika, the cross, the properties of stones and rings, divination, numbers, the Kabbalah, and ancient astrology.

## Arabic and Islamic learned magic

[Echoes of Niranj Magic in the Work of Albert the Great](2024_Rinotas_Echoes_of_Niranj_Magic_in_the_Work_of_Albert_the_Great.pdf) (2024), Athanasios Rinotas, Mediterranea 9, pp. 271-292. Niranj magic is an Arabic genre tied to the Pseudo-Aristotelian Hermetica whose name was never translated into Latin; the paper traces echoes of it in Albert the Great, as a sequel to Charles Burnett's earlier study.

[Al-Tabari on the Prayers to the Planets](1992_Pingree_Al-Tabari_on_the_Prayers_to_the_Planets.pdf) (1992), David Pingree, Bulletin d'etudes orientales 44, pp. 105-117. Examines the chapter of the Ghayat al-hakim (known in Latin as the Picatrix) that describes how the Sabians of Harran petitioned the planets and their spirits, and shows from a Latin translation that only a small part of it derives from al-Tabari, with the rest drawn mainly from other sources.

[Al-'Usur al-Wusta, volume 29](2021_Al-Usur_al-Wusta_Vol_29_Journal_of_Middle_East_Medievalists.pdf) (2021), The Journal of Middle East Medievalists, a complete 392-page issue. Articles include Liana Saif's long preliminary study of the Pseudo-Aristotelian Hermetica (pp. 20-80), Ahmad Al-Jallad on a digraph in the Quranic consonantal text, David Durand-Guedy on a Saljuq Anatolian ribat and its woman patron, and review essays and book reviews on medieval Islamic history, art, and book culture.

## Philosophy and the study of time

[Review of Taube, The Logika of the Judaizers](2019_Danylenko_Review_of_Taube_The_Logika_of_the_Judaizers_Slavonic_and_East_European_Review.pdf) (2019), Andrii Danylenko, Slavonic and East European Review 97(2). Reviews Moshe Taube's critical edition of a fifteenth-century Ruthenian translation of Hebrew logical texts (attributed to Maimonides and al-Ghazali), calling it masterfully crafted but flawed in its treatment of the Slavic language. The last page begins the next review in the journal.

[Contemplating Eternity: On Time, Death, and Timelessness in Ancient India](2019_Yanchevskaya_Contemplating_Eternity_On_Time_Death_and_Timelessness_in_Ancient_India_Harvard_Dissertation.pdf) (2019), Nataliya Yanchevskaya, Harvard doctoral dissertation. Follows concepts and myths of time from the Rgveda through the Upanisads, the epics, and the philosophical schools, arguing that even the earliest texts distinguish an eternal time from a transitory, profane one, and that time is treated as lord of the universe.

[The Creative Mind](1946_Bergson_The_Creative_Mind_Translated_by_Andison.pdf) (English edition 1946), Henri Bergson, translated by Mabelle L. Andison. Essays and lectures from 1903 to 1923, including the two introductions on the growth of truth and the stating of problems, the possible and the real, philosophical intuition, the perception of change, the introduction to metaphysics, and studies of Claude Bernard, William James, and Ravaisson.

[The Universe as a Cyclic Organized Information System: John Wheeler's World Revisited](2015_Meijer_The_Universe_as_a_Cyclic_Organized_Information_System_John_Wheelers_World_Revisited.pdf) (2015), Dirk K. F. Meijer, NeuroQuantology 13(1), pp. 57-78. An essay on Wheeler's participatory universe, in which reality grows out of observation and consciousness, information is its most fundamental building block, and the universe is a self-excited circuit developing through a closed-loop cycle.
