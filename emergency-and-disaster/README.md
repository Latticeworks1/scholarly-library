# Emergency and Disaster

Hurricane Katrina response reports, disaster communications, emergency call location and accessibility, and a mesh networking stack for resilient communication.

## Hurricane Katrina

[Hurricane Katrina After Action Review](2006_CDC_Hurricane_Katrina_After_Action_Review.pdf) (February 1, 2006), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, prepared for the director of its Coordinating Office for Terrorism Preparedness and Emergency Response. A 223-page review of the agency's Katrina response, organized as strengths, areas for improvement, and a corrective action plan. This is a FOIA release with redactions.

[Hurricane Katrina After Action Report, OR-2 DMAT at the New Orleans Airport](2005_OR-2_DMAT_Hurricane_Katrina_After_Action_Report_New_Orleans_Airport.pdf) (September 25, 2005). A 16-page report by the OR-2 disaster medical assistance team on its deployment to the New Orleans airport from August 31 to September 10, 2005, written by its team commander, deputy commander, and medical officer, with observations from other team members.

[Medical Treatment at Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport after Hurricane Katrina](2007_Sanford_Jui_Miller_Jobe_Medical_Treatment_at_Louis_Armstrong_New_Orleans_Airport_After_Hurricane_Katrina.pdf) (2007), Christopher Sanford, Jonathan Jui, Helen Miller, and Kathleen Jobe, Travel Medicine and Infectious Disease 5, pp. 230-235. Describes the airport triage and treatment station from which over 3,000 patients were evacuated by air in the week after the storm, the largest air evacuation in history.

## Disaster communications and technology

[TeamPhone: Networking Smartphones for Disaster Recovery](2017_Lu_Cao_La_Porta_TeamPhone_Networking_Smartphones_for_Disaster_Recovery.pdf) (2017), Zongqing Lu, Guohong Cao, and Thomas La Porta, arXiv 1606.03417v2. A system that joins cellular, ad-hoc, and opportunistic networking into a messaging system for rescue workers, plus a self-rescue system that groups trapped survivors' phones and sends energy-efficient emergency messages with their locations; prototyped on Android.

[Harnessing Mobile Technology for Flood Disaster Readiness and Response: A Comprehensive Review of Mobile Applications on the Google Play Store](2025_Kangana_et_al_Harnessing_Mobile_Technology_for_Flood_Disaster_Readiness_and_Response_A_Comprehensive_Review_of_Mobile_Applications_on_the_Google_Play_Store.pdf) (2025), Nuwani Kangana and colleagues, Urban Science 9(4). Identifies and evaluates 77 disaster-related apps on the Google Play Store and recommends improving functionality and accuracy, using crowdsourcing to enrich data, and building in long-term sustainability.

[Reticulum Network Stack Manual, Release 1.3.4](2026_Qvist_Reticulum_Network_Stack_Manual_Release_1.3.4.pdf) (May 29, 2026), Mark Qvist. The 263-page manual for a networking stack in which every communication rests on X25519 encryption and Ed25519 signatures, low-bandwidth links coexist with high-bandwidth ones, an encrypted verified link costs three packets totalling 297 bytes, and the network can also run encapsulated over existing IP networks.

## Emergency calls and public safety (EENA)

These are publications of the European Emergency Number Association, which represents emergency services organizations.

[AML Report Card, 2023 Update](2023_EENA_AML_Report_Card_Advanced_Mobile_Location_Deployment_in_30_Countries.pdf) (2023). The third edition of the survey of Advanced Mobile Location, the handset-based technology that sends a caller's position to emergency services. For 30 countries it covers how messages are transmitted and how fast they arrive, roaming, text-to-112, and the effect on emergency services and citizens.

[112 Indoors: Improving Emergency Response with Indoor Location and Indoor Maps](2026_EENA_112_Indoors_Improving_Emergency_Response_with_Indoor_Location_and_Indoor_Maps.pdf) (June 2, 2026). Explains that a growing share of calls to 112 come from mobile phones inside buildings, where location is hard to determine, and that even available indoor location data is of limited use in multi-story or complex buildings unless it is accurate.

[Emergency Communications over LTE and IMS: Unresolved Issues and Risks](2026_EENA_Emergency_Communications_over_LTE_and_IMS_Unresolved_Issues_and_Risks.pdf) (February 2, 2026). Emergency calls over LTE and IP-based systems suffer from failed roaming, missing caller identification, and blocked callbacks, largely because testing across networks, devices, and emergency call centers is incomplete.

[Implementation Strategies for Accessible Emergency Services](2025_EENA_Implementation_Strategies_for_Accessible_Emergency_Services.pdf) (July 8, 2025). Examines how Sweden, the Netherlands, and France use Total Conversation and other inclusive technologies to give deaf, deafblind, and hard-of-hearing people equal access to 112.

[AI Special Project Final Report: Assessing How AI Can Improve Public Safety](2024_EENA_AI_Special_Project_Final_Report_Assessing_How_AI_Can_Improve_Public_Safety.pdf) (December 5, 2024). Reports pilots run with emergency call centers on language transcription and translation, noise reduction and audio filtering, and hints and recommendations for call-takers, followed by recommendations.
